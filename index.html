Prototype plan

For approval before UI build. No styling decisions have been made here.

Product

01

Daily case

  • One case study a day. Read it once, then answer it from three angles: VARC, DI/LR, QA.
  • Short, real, CAT-level. Modelled on Cracku's free daily set, except the three questions share one case instead of sitting unrelated.
  • No login. No email. No OTP. Open the link, answer.
  • Free navigation across the three. No skip button, since a daily set of three does not need one.
  • Expires at midnight. Same discipline as Cracku's 24-hour window.

Cracku gates the same offer behind a mandatory OTP wall. R01 called that gated tool the best free thing he used. IIMs run case comps across disciplines, so prep runs the same way.

02

Weekly mock

  • Interface-faithful. Laptop only, built to match the real exam environment rather than copy anyone's software.
  • Full sectional timing, 40 minutes a section, no pausing, no going back across sections.
  • Question level synced to where the student currently sits, not a fixed national paper.
  • Weekly, because toppers median 37 mocks and two in three take 20 to 45.

R02: mock environment feels nothing like the real exam, nerves hit in the final. R01 gave 25 to 30 mocks and still landed 80 percentile.

03

Error tagging and review

  • Every wrong answer is tagged by the platform, not by the student.
  • Four tags, held in the backend: concept gap, method error, calculation slip, selection error.
  • Tag derived from behaviour. Time to first interaction, whether a method was started, where the chain broke, prior attempts on the same concept.
  • The student never sees the taxonomy. Jacob's law: show them the stats they already read on every other platform.

Concept gapTopic marked not started. Routed to the concept repo.
Method errorTime above cohort average on a correct-method question. Shown an alternative approach.

R02, amended item 15: the assignment of weak points should come from the platform, not from instinct. R01 keeps only topic names, with nothing recorded about what goes wrong inside them. Nothing in the teardown reports cause of error.

04

Weak point read

  • Topics carry their failure mode, not just their name.
  • RC splits into comprehension and conclusion. Jumbled paragraphs splits into sentence formation and opening sentence.
  • Repeat errors flagged by count, since a topic missed twice behaves differently from a topic missed once.
  • Built from attempt data. Nothing for the student to fill in.

R01 writes the topic name and nothing else, then named logarithms and functions as errors he expects to repeat five or six times. R03 named logarithms and statistics as still unresolved.

05

1:1 mentorship, credit based

  • Mentors are current students at the colleges the user is targeting. Sourced through the Meridian network and the IIM networks reachable through it.
  • Model follows mentorunion.org. One credit buys half an hour. Each student starts with 10 credits.
  • Mentor supply is the constraint, so credits are earned back through streaks, mock completion and referrals rather than bought.
  • Covers both halves: concept 1:1 for the test, and GD/PI practice for the interview.

66% of R01's 500 to 600 strong TIME batch left straight after the test and never returned for interview prep. The interview is where tier 2 and 3 candidates lose the seat they cleared the test for.

06

Concept repos

  • First-principles breakdowns, written, not filmed. Video time tracks downward against score.
  • Organised by the framework underneath the question rather than by chapter, since CAT tests structure identification before it tests arithmetic.
  • Reached from an error tag, not browsed. The repo opens because something was missed, not because it is next on a syllabus.

R02: concept faults come from early classes nobody pays attention to. R03 is not mathematical and found the standard Indian method of study a poor fit.

Onboarding

07

Rolling, optional, never finished

  • Not required. The daily case runs for anyone, forever, with zero information handed over.
  • Sign-up is prompted on every visit, present but not in the face.
  • No upfront form. Questions arrive across sessions, each one attached to a feature at the moment it is discovered.
  • Profile questions: 10th, 12th, graduation, stream, college, category, gender, attempt number, target schools.
  • Work experience in pegs, not free text: 1 month, 3, 6, 12, 24, then years. Built for freshers and near-freshers.
  • One competitor question: coaching, purchased mocks, online course, or nothing.

R03 registered on time and started three weeks out with no idea where to begin. R01's plan came entirely from his institute. R02 reports the students who set their own plan were always the top performers.

08

Profile read

  • Onboarding answers score the user against the profile of candidates who actually convert, by institute, since each IIM weights academics differently.
  • Students close to a real shot get told so, and get mentor credits pushed at them.
  • Students far from it get told what the gap is in plain terms, including when the answer is work experience rather than a higher percentile.
  • Error tags feed the same read. What the platform promotes to a user is decided by where that user actually faults.

R01 quit 10 to 12 days out after working out his own odds unaided: no category, no work ex, ordinary percentages. His own recommendation was that a fresher who cannot reach 99 should take 2 to 3 years of work experience first. Students are ROI decision-makers.

Home

09

Stats strip

  • Very small section. Motivating numbers only.
  • Live and specific: how many answered today's case, average time, how the user's band moved.
  • Tell the user when they are the most active, or the strongest answerer, in the current window.
  • Benchmarks pulled from the band just above the user. No 99 percentile anchors.

Every platform in the teardown anchors its social proof on toppers who were already strong before they joined. R02: what nearly made them stop was grades not improving.

Backend

10

Data, inside one hour and zero spend

  • Anonymous play is local. Attempts, timings and streak held in the browser, keyed to a generated ID.
  • On sign-up the local record is posted to a Google Sheet through an Apps Script endpoint. Free, no infrastructure, no account for the user to manage.
  • Sheet holds: anon ID, profile answers, per-question timings, tags, credits, mentor sessions.
  • Content is static JSON in the repo, versioned by date. The daily case ships as a file, not a database row.
  • Supabase is out. It cannot be stood up end to end in the time available.

Go to market

11

Who, and when

  • Primary target is the post-result window, not the 30 days before. Exam clearers awaiting calls, specifically private-college GEMs and high-diversity non-engineers holding strong composite scores and highly risk-averse.
  • Secondary is the next cycle's takers, who are deciding on coaching right now.
  • Both convert at zero cost, since both are already looking.
12

Profiling as the growth engine

  • Sign up candidates closest to the real IIM ICP, build them into winner cohorts, let the results carry the word of mouth.
  • Selection rate is the metric, so the admissions bar at intake is a growth lever, not a filter.
13

City mixers

  • GD and interview prep in person, run where the Meridian network overlaps the local candidate pool.
  • Pilot in metros. Kill the format if sign-ups come in under 10 per mixer.
  • One mentor per mixer, drawn from the same credit pool.
  • Zero spend. Public venue, no booking, no catering.
Out of the MVP. Video lessons, live classes, doubt forum, paid mock series, application form cross-sell.

Unresolved. How the error tag behaves before enough attempt data exists. Whether a student accepts being told their weakness rather than naming it. Whether no-login content retains anyone, given 90% of students handed a free proven tool used it under six hours. Whether mentor supply holds at 100 mentors against the credit load.
