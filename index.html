Prototype plan

What each screen will contain, for approval before the UI build. No styling decisions made here.

Core loop

Screen 01

Today's set, no login

Entry point. Reachable by anyone with the link.

  • Three items dated to today: one VARC, one DILR set, one Quant.
  • Start button leads straight into the question. No account, no email, no OTP.
  • Progress held in the browser until the user chooses to save it.
  • One line stating what is kept and what is not.

Cracku gates the identical offer behind a mandatory OTP wall. R01 named that gated tool the best free thing he used, and still described online prep as a waste of time.

Screen 02

Attempting a question

The exam surface.

  • Question, options, running timer.
  • Skip is a first-class action sitting beside Submit, not hidden.
  • Time to first interaction recorded silently.
  • Time at the moment of skip recorded silently.

R01 decides to skip inside 20 to 30 seconds. R02 decides within the first few words. Neither platform records that decision, so neither student can review it.

Screen 03

After a wrong answer

The screen the whole product turns on.

  • The correct answer and the method.
  • The platform states the cause. Concept gap, method error, calculation slip, or selection error.
  • Cause derived from behaviour, not self-report: time taken, whether the method was started, where the chain broke, prior attempts on the same concept.
  • The student can disagree in one tap, which trains the assignment.
  • Each cause routes somewhere different, rather than all roads leading to a video.

R02 item 15, amended: the identification of weak points should come from the platform, not from instinct. R01 keeps a list of topic names with nothing inside them. No platform walked tonight reports cause of error.

Screen 04

Weak-point map

What the student actually has, instead of a list of topic names.

  • Topics listed with the failure mode inside each one.
  • RC splits into comprehension failure and conclusion failure, rather than reading "RC".
  • Jumbled paragraphs splits into sentence formation and opening sentence.
  • Topics marked by whether the same error has recurred, and how many times.
  • Built entirely from attempt data. Nothing to fill in.

R01 writes only the topic name and says the name alone tells him where he is going wrong. He then named logarithms and functions as errors he expects to repeat five or six times. R03 named logarithms and statistics as still unresolved.

Retention

Screen 05

Rolling onboarding

Onboarding that never formally ends.

  • No upfront form. The first session is a question, not a questionnaire.
  • Each unseen feature carries its own onboarding moment, completed on discovery.
  • Questions asked across sessions build the profile: attempt history, stream, work experience, target schools.
  • Those answers feed a readiness read against the profile of students who actually convert.

R03 had no idea where to start and prepared alone for three weeks. R01's plan came entirely from his institute. R02 reports that students who set their own plan were always the top performers.

Screen 06

Session timing

When the student is prompted, and why then.

  • Accuracy and speed logged against hour of day and day of week.
  • Prompts land in the windows where that individual performs, not on a fixed schedule.
  • Session length adapts to how the current session is going.
  • The student can see the read and override it.

TIME ran five hours a day on a fixed timetable. R01 studied four to five hours, scored 80 percentile, and said the volume stopped making sense. No platform in the teardown adapts to time of day or pace.

Screen 07

Where you stand

Progress against a capped seat count.

  • Position against the cohort, not a personal score curve.
  • Movement shown as how the field moved, since percentile is relative.
  • Benchmarks drawn from the band just above the student, not from the 99th percentile.

R02: the thing that nearly made them stop was grades not improving in mocks. R01 quit 10 to 12 days out, once he worked out what his profile could realistically reach. Every competitor anchors its social proof on 99 percentile scorers.

Not in the MVP. Video lessons, live classes, a doubt forum, mock test series, interview prep, city case competitions, offline meetups. Each is either covered well by an incumbent or sits outside the 30 days.

Deliberately unresolved. How cause-of-error assignment behaves before enough attempt data exists. How much a student will accept being told their own weakness. Whether no-login content retains or evaporates.
